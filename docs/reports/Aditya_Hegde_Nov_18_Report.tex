\section{Problem Formulation \& Causal Filter Failure}
In industrial Cyber-Physical Systems (CPS) \cite{cps_survey}, adversarial additive spoofing corrupts sensor telemetry at discrete timesteps $t \in \{1, \dots, T\}$ according to $y_t = x_t + \delta_t$, where $x_t \in \mathbb{R}$ is the unobserved physical plant state and $\delta_t \in \mathbb{R}$ is the unknown attack perturbation ($\delta_t = 0$ during clean operations). Recovering both trajectories from telemetry $y$ alone is non-identifiable without side information or structural priors. Standard causal estimators (Persistence, Seasonal Naive) fail under sustained spoofing because corrupted measurements directly contaminate their autoregressive states, driving severe divergence (Persistence median NRMSE $0.8616$; Seasonal Naive $1.4802$).

\section{Decoupled Inversion via Multi-Order Trend Filtering}
To restore identifiability without ground truth at test time, we decouple the attacked channel from its corrupted history using uncompromised companion channels $z_t \in \mathbb{R}^d$ (flows, valve positions, adjacent pressures). A causal neural surrogate \cite{tcn} yields clean state estimates $\hat{x}_{\text{surr}, t} = f_\theta(z_{t-W:t-1})$ with empirical surrogate residual variance $\sigma_\epsilon^2 = \mathbb{E}[(x_t - \hat{x}_{\text{surr}, t})^2]$, where $\epsilon_t = x_t - \hat{x}_{\text{surr}, t}$ represents nominal surrogate tracking error. To recover complex, continuous attack profiles (steps, ramps, physical drift), we formulate a box-constrained Multi-Order Trend Filter \cite{trend,fused} solved via the Alternating Direction Method of Multipliers (ADMM) \cite{admm}:
\begin{equation}
\min_{\delta \in \mathbb{R}^T} \;\; \frac{1}{2\sigma_\epsilon^2} \| y - \hat{x}_{\text{surr}} - \delta \|_2^2 + \lambda_0 \|\delta\|_1 + \lambda_1 \|\mathbf{D}^{(1)}\delta\|_1 + \lambda_2 \|\mathbf{D}^{(2)}\delta\|_1 \quad \text{s.t.} \quad y - \delta \in [x_{\min}, x_{\max}]
\end{equation}
The $\ell_1$ penalty enforces temporal sparsity; $\mathbf{D}^{(1)}$ penalizes abrupt step/ramp transitions; and $\mathbf{D}^{(2)}$ tracks smooth physical drift, while physical box constraints guarantee zero envelope violations.

\section{Multi-Testbed Empirical Benchmarks}
Scaling beyond our initial single-tank BATADAL \cite{batadal} prototype, we evaluated the framework across three industrial testbeds: BATADAL (43 channels), SWaT \cite{swat} (51 channels), and WADI \cite{wadi} (122 channels). Multi-channel CausalTCN surrogates (trained on TACC Vista GH200 nodes; 100k parameters, OneCycleLR \cite{onecycle}) compressed residual standard deviations to $0.0867\,\text{m}$ (BATADAL \texttt{L\_T1}), $9.5677\,\text{mm}$ (SWaT \texttt{LIT101}), and $5.7187\,\text{mm}$ (WADI \texttt{1\_LT\_001\_PV}). We benchmarked 5 canonical attack families (step, ramp, drift, pulse, periodic across 50 scenarios per dataset) and evaluated high-throughput stress sweeps across 3,000 randomized scenarios (1,000 per dataset).
